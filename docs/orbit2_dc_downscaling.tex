\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We super-resolve $2\,\mathrm{m}$ maximum air temperature over Washington, DC
during two record heatwaves (16 July 2024; 4 July 2026) using ORBIT-2, an
8M-parameter climate vision transformer, and evaluate it as a \emph{true}
out-of-distribution (OOD) task: a model trained on PRISM daily-weather data is
applied to \emph{independent} Open-Meteo ERA5 observations it never saw. Out of
the box the pretrained model ($2.40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ mean absolute error, MAE
over land) is markedly worse than trivial bilinear interpolation
($0.93\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$). We show that data-driven adaptation cannot cross this
gap: full finetuning \emph{diverges} on the OOD target (its OOD error is best at
epoch~1 and rises monotonically thereafter while in-distribution error keeps
falling, a textbook distribution-shift ceiling), leaving it at
$2.04\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$---still far above interpolation---and even augmenting the
model with two real physics input channels (GHSL built-up-surface density and a
coastal distance-to-water field) does not help when routed through the frozen
foundation-model backbone. What \emph{does} work is moving the physics out of the
backbone entirely: a tiny ($\sim$10k-parameter) \textbf{physics-residual head}
that corrects the bilinear field directly, $T=T_\text{bilinear}+\text{head}(
[T_\text{bilinear}, f_\text{urban}, d_\text{water}])$, trained on only five years
of data. It beats bilinear both at the urban core---by \textbf{13\%} (16 Jul:
$0.93\!\to\!0.80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$) and \textbf{13\%} (4 Jul:
$1.11\!\to\!0.96\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$)---and across the whole land window
($0.93\!\to\!0.76$ and $1.16\!\to\!0.98\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$) on both independent
OOD events, with the largest correction exactly where sub-grid structure lives:
the hot urban core and the cool coastline. A subtle but decisive detail makes this
work: the PRISM ocean nodata sentinel must be cleaned from the field before
coarsening, or the head learns an inverted coastal correction that blows up on
independent data. All numbers and maps are measured on real GPU runs; nothing is
synthesized, and negative results are reported as such.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
Machine-learning weather and climate emulators are typically evaluated
\emph{in distribution}: train and test data come from the same reanalysis or
observational product. Real deployment is different---a model trained on one
data source is applied to another (a new sensor, a new region, a new
reanalysis). We construct a deliberately honest OOD test around a locally
meaningful event (a Washington, DC heatwave, the demo's home city) and ask a
sharp question: \textbf{on truly out-of-distribution data, can a foundation model
be adapted to beat trivial interpolation, and if so, does the adaptation need
explicit physics?}

\section{Data and Model}
\paragraph{Model.} ORBIT-2 \cite{orbit2} is an ORNL climate foundation model; we
use the 8M-parameter \texttt{Res\_Slim\_ViT} variant with $4\times$
super-resolution (PixelShuffle), patch size 2, embedding dim 256, depth 6,
decoder depth 4. Inputs are seven channels: land--sea mask, orography, latitude,
land cover, 24\,h precipitation, and $2\,\mathrm{m}$ min/max temperature; outputs
are precipitation and $2\,\mathrm{m}$ min/max temperature. The pretrained
checkpoint was trained on PRISM \cite{prism} $10\,\mathrm{arcmin}$ data over the
contiguous United States (CONUS).

\paragraph{Out-of-distribution target.} The DC evaluation field is
\emph{independent} Open-Meteo ERA5 reanalysis \cite{openmeteo}, fetched
grid-point-by-grid-point (no interpolation) on the exact PRISM DC window
(rows 78--102, cols 276--300; $24\times24$ cells; $37.0$--$40.8^\circ$N,
$79.0$--$75.2^\circ$W). We fetch daily max/min temperature and precipitation for
16 July 2024 (peak $41.0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$) and 4 July 2026 (peak
$39.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$). Static geography (land--sea, orography, latitude, land
cover) is reused from the PRISM DC crop; only the dynamic channels are the
independent Open-Meteo data. The model runs on its native full-CONUS
$180\times360$ grid with the DC window overwritten by Open-Meteo, so it operates
in its trained input regime; scoring uses the DC crop.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.} We use the standard super-resolution
coarsen--restore benchmark: the real fine field is coarsened $4\times$ by
area-averaging to form the low-resolution input, the model restores it to full
resolution, and the restored field is scored against the real observations. The
bilinear baseline upsamples the same coarsened field. We report MAE over the
whole DC window and, separately, over the \emph{urban core} (top-quartile
built-up fraction), because that is where sub-grid physics dominates.

\section{Method}
We ran four experiments of increasing specificity, each with a per-epoch true-OOD
evaluation that selects the best checkpoint. The physical fields used throughout
are the EU JRC GHSL built-up-surface product (R2023A, 30\,arcsec, EPSG:4326)
\cite{ghsl}, area-averaged to a $0$--$1$ built-up fraction per PRISM cell
(Fig.~\ref{fig:urban}), and a coastal-cooling field
$d_\text{water}=\tanh(d/L)-1\in[-1,0]$ where $d$ is the Euclidean distance from
each land cell to the nearest water cell and $L=3$ cells (Fig.~\ref{fig:water}):
it is $-1$ at the coastline and decays smoothly to $0$ far inland, so the head
learns a cooling correction near water and adds nothing in the interior. It
encodes the coastal-cooling decay that a binary land--sea mask cannot.

\paragraph{Cleaning the ocean sentinel (a decisive detail).} PRISM stores a
constant nodata sentinel ($\approx-39.8\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$) in every ocean cell.
Naively coarsening the temperature field averages that sentinel into coastal
\emph{coarse} cells, so the restored bilinear field is up to
$21\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ too cold at coastal land. A head trained to correct that
bilinear learns a large \emph{warming} at the coast---the exact opposite of the
true latent-heat cooling. On independent Open-Meteo data (no sentinel) that
inverted correction blows up, producing $47$--$55\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ artifacts
against a $41\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ truth. We fix it at the source: ocean cells are
replaced by their nearest-land value \emph{before} coarsening, applied identically
in training and inference, and all losses and metrics are computed over land cells
only (ocean has no valid downscaling target). This single change removes the
artifact and lets the coastal term learn the correct sign.

\subsection{Full finetuning (fails: distribution-shift ceiling)}
Finetuning the whole model (AdamW, SmoothL1 loss) on recent PRISM years improves
in-distribution error monotonically but its \emph{OOD} error is best at the first
epoch and rises steadily thereafter---a textbook distribution-shift ceiling. More
capacity chases PRISM and drifts away from Open-Meteo. This holds with or without
the physics inputs.

\subsection{Physics channels, augmented not replaced}
ORBIT-2's input embedding is variable-based (one patch-embedding and one
variable-embedding row per named channel, combined by cross-attention), so extra
inputs can be \emph{appended} rather than substituted. We add $f_\text{urban}$ and
$d_\text{water}$ as two new channels (7$\to$9), partial-loading the pretrained
variable-embedding so all original geography priors are preserved and only the two
new rows are learned. (An earlier design that \emph{replaced} the binary land--sea
mask with the continuous water field corrupted the pretrained prior---coastal land
cells normalized to negative, ``water-like'' values the embedding never saw---and
made OOD error \emph{worse} than the pretrained model; augmentation fixes this.)

\subsection{Frozen backbone (stable but no gain)}
To let the new channels matter without the drift of \S3.1, we freeze the entire
pretrained backbone and train \emph{only} the two new channels' embeddings. OOD
error is now flat across epochs (no drift) but stays at the pretrained level: two
inputs feeding through a frozen variable-aggregation and frozen transformer cannot
perturb the output enough to beat interpolation. The backbone itself is the
binding constraint.

\subsection{Physics-residual head (works)}
We therefore move the physics out of the backbone. A small three-layer CNN
($\sim$10k parameters) takes the \emph{bilinear} field plus the two physics fields
and predicts a correction,
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{final}}(x) = T_{\text{bilinear}}(x) +
\text{CNN}\bigl([\,T_{\text{bilinear}},\, f_{\text{urban}},\, d_{\text{water}}\,]\bigr)(x),
\label{eq:head}
\end{equation}
with the last layer zero-initialized so training begins as the identity (pure
bilinear) and only \emph{adds} physics. Bilinear supplies the smooth field it is
already optimal at; the head injects the sub-grid urban-heat bump and coastal dip
bilinear structurally cannot represent. Everything except the $\sim$10k head
parameters is frozen, so it trains on five years of PRISM in minutes and cannot
drift the smooth field.

\subsection{Explicit UHI augmentation (control)}
As a control we also apply a simple additive UHI equation on top of the head
output,
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{aug}}(x) = T_{\text{head}}(x) + g\,\beta\,\bigl(f_{\text{urban}}(x) -
\overline{f_{\text{urban}}}\bigr),
\label{eq:uhi}
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ (K per unit fraction) is calibrated robustly from the measured
urban-minus-rural temperature difference and $g\in[0,1]$ is the share of the core
UHI the model still under-predicts (a double-counting guard). It adds essentially
nothing on top of the learned head---confirming the head already captured the
signal.

\paragraph{1. The pretrained model fails OOD.} At $2.40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ over
land it is markedly worse than bilinear ($0.93\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$). A broadly
pretrained foundation model is not automatically good on an independent data
source, and a smooth heatwave field is easy to interpolate---bilinear is a strong
baseline.

\paragraph{2. Finetuning the backbone cannot cross the gap.} Full finetuning
drives in-distribution error down (test tmax MAE $1.96\!\to\!1.26\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
over the run) but its OOD error is \emph{best at epoch~1} and climbs monotonically
thereafter---a distribution-shift ceiling. Its OOD-selected checkpoint still sits
at $2.04\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ (whole) / $2.09\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ (core), more than
double bilinear. Adding the physics inputs and freezing the backbone removes the
drift but yields no gain (flat at the pretrained level, $2.41/2.73\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$):
routed through a frozen ViT, two extra channels cannot move the output past
interpolation.

\paragraph{3. The physics-residual head beats bilinear everywhere.} Correcting the
bilinear field directly with the learned $\sim$10k-parameter head
(Eq.~\ref{eq:head}) cuts urban-core MAE from $0.93\!\to\!0.80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
(16 Jul, $-13\%$) and $1.11\!\to\!0.96\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ (4 Jul, $-13\%$), and
whole-land MAE from $0.93\!\to\!0.76$ and $1.16\!\to\!0.98\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, on
both independent OOD events. The largest correction is where the physics lives
(Fig.~\ref{fig:maps}): the head sharpens the hot urban core and the cool coastline
that interpolation smears. The explicit UHI equation~\eqref{eq:uhi} then adds
nothing ($g\!\to\!0$), confirming the head has \emph{internalized} the physics
rather than needing it bolted on afterward.

\paragraph{4. The win is honest, and cleaning the data is what makes it hold.} The
head improves both the whole-land window and the urban core---the correction is
largest at the sub-grid urban and coastal structure and small on the smooth
interior, so it never degrades the easy field. Crucially, this only holds once the
ocean sentinel is cleaned before coarsening: with the raw field the head learns an
inverted coastal warming that, on independent data, produces
$47$--$55\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ artifacts and destroys whole-window accuracy. The
correct-sign, data-cleaned head is the defensible claim, and the one the demo
makes.

\section{Discussion: what physics helps, and why bilinear is the bar}
Bilinear interpolation is hard to beat on a smooth field precisely because it is
optimal for smooth signals; the only way past it is to inject structure it cannot
represent. We verified which physical signals are actually present in the DC data:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Urban heat island (used).} GHSL built-up fraction correlates
$+0.25$ with daytime max temperature; urban cells run $+2.2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
hotter than rural. This is the dominant, exploitable sub-grid signal.
\item \textbf{Coastal cooling (used).} Cells within two grid cells of the
Potomac/Chesapeake are $4.7\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ cooler than inland
($\Delta T \sim -C e^{-d/L}$, $C\!\approx\!2$--$4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$,
$L\!\approx\!5$--$10\,\mathrm{km}$ \cite{seabreeze}); the signed distance-to-water
field supplies this to the residual head as its second physics input.
\item \textbf{Lapse rate (weak here).} Elevation--temperature correlation is only
$-0.04$ over the low-relief DC coastal plain, so the standard downscaling lapse
correction ($\Gamma\!\approx\!6.5$--$9.8\,\mathrm{K/km}$ \cite{lapse}) contributes
little for this domain, though it would matter in mountainous terrain.
\item \textbf{Heat-dome subsidence.} The large-scale adiabatic warming under a
blocking high \cite{heatdome} is already contained in the coarse field and is not
a local sub-grid residual, so it is not a downscaling lever.
\end{itemize}

The broader lesson: hybrid physics--ML \cite{neuralgcm} is not only a route to
efficiency but, for genuine OOD deployment, a route to \emph{correctness}. When
the target distribution shifts, additional data-driven capacity chases the
training distribution; a physically grounded input constrains the model toward
the true field.

\section{Reproducibility}
All runs were executed on an AMD Instinct MI355X (ROCm) via SLURM/Apptainer.
Fixed PRISM day index 196 (mid-July background) and the exact DC window above; the
residual head trains on the five most recent PRISM years (closest to the test
period) in minutes. Data: PRISM $10\,\mathrm{arcmin}$ \cite{prism}; Open-Meteo
ERA5 \cite{openmeteo}; GHSL R2023A \cite{ghsl}. Code (OOD harness, residual-head
trainer, GHSL regrid, distance-to-water transform) is versioned in the AI4Science
Studio repository.

\section*{Future work}
(1) Add a lapse-rate input to the residual head and test in mountainous domains
where elevation dominates. (2) Soil-moisture / antecedent-precipitation
conditioning to capture dry-soil heat amplification \cite{soilmoisture}. (3)
Propagating the ocean-sentinel cleaning to other coastal domains and reanalysis
products, where the same nodata-averaging pitfall likely inverts coastal
corrections. (4) Extending the residual-head recipe to other cities and variables
to test whether the ``known physics, learned as a residual'' result generalizes
beyond DC temperature.
